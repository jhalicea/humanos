## 3. Role model

The role sits at the intersection of:

**CONTENT PROVIDER / BROKER / SELL-SIDE INSTITUTION**  
→ contribution / dissemination system  
→ Bloomberg content acquisition and support workflow  
→ validation / timeliness / quality  
→ Bloomberg research platform / downstream products  
→ Bloomberg clients consuming the content

The Technical Account Manager is not only a help-desk technician and not only an account manager. The core operating loop is:

**UNDERSTAND CLIENT → UNDERSTAND SYSTEM → OWN ISSUE → USE DATA → COORDINATE TEAMS → IMPROVE WORKFLOW → STRENGTHEN RELATIONSHIP**

## 4. Job-derived skill map

The course adds the following competency cluster to the Academy:

1. **Financial-market structure** — buy side, sell side, brokers, exchanges, research providers, equities, market participants, information flows.
2. **Research-data ecosystem** — research contributors/providers, dissemination, discoverability, entitlements, timeliness, client value.
3. **Content dissemination and provider data flows** — source systems, ingestion, transformation, validation, delivery, downstream use and failure points.
4. **Data quality and delivery validation** — completeness, timeliness, consistency, duplicates/errors, reconciliation, escalation.
5. **SQL data analysis** — filtering, joins, grouping, aggregation, quality checks, trend analysis.
6. **Python for analysis/automation** — reading data, cleaning, validating, comparing, summarizing and automating repetitive checks.
7. **Business intelligence** — metrics, trends, gaps, segmentation, dashboards, product/content improvement.
8. **Workflow automation** — identify repetitive work, map triggers/data/actions, error handling, reliability and measurable improvement.
9. **Customer service** — clarify, own, communicate, resolve/escalate, close the loop.
10. **Relationship management** — trust, discovery, expectation-setting, difficult conversations, follow-through and account health.
11. **Technical account management** — understand client architecture and business goals, translate needs into technical action, coordinate implementation/support and advise on improvements.
12. **Ticket/SLA operations** — queue hygiene, prioritization, SLA awareness, triage, ownership, escalation, status updates and closure quality.
13. **Account/portfolio strategy** — client-touch planning, goals, metrics, proactive value, presence/distribution improvement and outcome tracking.
14. **Multi-project prioritization** — urgency vs impact, dependencies, queue management, stakeholder alignment and reliable follow-through.
15. **Continuous improvement** — challenge the status quo constructively, test improvements, document results and scale what works.
16. **Stakeholder communication** — concise technical explanation, client/executive updates, active listening, expectation management and negotiation.
17. **Cross-functional/global collaboration** — Product, Technical Account Management, Content, Engineering and Operations coordination.
18. **Systems/API/database fluency** — enough technical depth to reason about custom content-delivery systems and troubleshoot interfaces without pretending to be the deepest engineer in the room.

Pre-hire preparation focuses on the underlying transferable behavior: ticket intake, prioritization, clear notes, ownership, SLA discipline, escalation and customer communication. ## 7. Five-Day Interview Sprint

### Day 1 — Role + Bloomberg Research Data Map
**Goal:** explain the job clearly without reading the posting.

Learn:
- Bloomberg Data / Content Acquisition role in broad terms;
- why institutions contribute content;
- why Bloomberg clients value consolidated content;
- broker / provider / contributor / sell-side / buy-side / equities / research vocabulary;
- the value exchange between provider, Bloomberg and end client.

Oral interview:
1. What do you think this role actually does?
2. Why would a data or research provider want its content distributed on Bloomberg?
3. What is the difference between a customer-service role and a Technical Account Manager role?

Hands-on:
- Draw a one-page provider → Bloomberg → client data-flow map.
- Mark business owner, technical owner, data-quality checks, failure points and communications.

Failure drill:
- A provider says, “Bloomberg is the problem.” You have incomplete evidence. Explain what you do before assigning blame.

Evidence:
- 90-second role explanation.
- Data-flow diagram.
- Vocabulary teach-back.

### Day 2 — Research Markets + Account Strategy
**Goal:** talk comfortably with Relationship Managers about the client/business side.

Learn:
- buy side vs sell side;
- equity research basics;
- research provider/distributor relationships;
- timeliness and discoverability of research;
- why content presence matters to a provider;
- account segmentation, touch cadence, goals and metrics.

Oral interview:
1. How would you learn the goals of a new research provider?
2. How would you create an account plan for a broker whose content is underused or inconsistently delivered?
3. How do you balance client value with Bloomberg's internal priorities?

Hands-on:
Create a one-page account plan:
- provider goals;
- current state;
- technical/data issues;
- relationship risks;
- measurable 30/60/90-day actions;
- internal partners needed.

Failure drill:
- Client wants a change Engineering cannot deliver immediately. Set expectations without damaging trust.

### Day 3 — Data Delivery, Quality, Tickets and SLAs
**Goal:** own an issue from first report to closure.

Learn:
- generic data-delivery pipeline concepts;
- timeliness/completeness/consistency/duplicates/schema or formatting failures;
- ticket hygiene;
- priority and impact;
- SLA concepts;
- escalation quality;
- client status updates.

Oral interview:
1. A provider's research delivery is delayed. What do you do?
2. What information must be in a strong escalation ticket?
3. How do you decide what to work on first when several accounts need help?

Hands-on:
Create a ticket from this structure:
**Issue → Impact → Scope → Evidence → Steps tried → Owner → Next action → ETA/commitment → Escalation → Client update → Closure criteria**

Failure drill:
- A ticket is technically resolved, but the client says the problem is not fixed. Diagnose the gap between technical closure and customer outcome.

### Day 4 — SQL, Python, BI and Automation Refresh
**Goal:** demonstrate analytical fluency without pretending to be a data engineer.

SQL refresh:
- SELECT / WHERE;
- JOIN;
- GROUP BY;
- COUNT / SUM / AVG;
- NULL handling;
- duplicate detection;
- date grouping;
- top/bottom contributors;
- delayed-delivery analysis.

Python refresh:
- read a CSV/JSON dataset;
- inspect columns/types;
- normalize dates;
- find missing/late/duplicate records;
- summarize by provider;
- produce an exception list.

BI thinking:
- define a useful metric;
- spot a trend;
- identify a gap;
- distinguish correlation from a client need;
- turn analysis into an action recommendation.

Automation exercise:
Design a workflow that checks provider deliveries, flags exceptions, opens/updates a support queue item and produces a daily summary. Include failure handling and human review.

Failure drill:
- Your automated alert begins generating false positives after a provider changes a field format. Diagnose and repair the workflow.

## 8. High-priority interview question bank

Practice until answers are conversational, not memorized.

### Role / motivation
- Walk me through your understanding of the Technical Account Manager - Research Data role.
- Why Bloomberg?
- Why Research Data rather than a pure software or pure account-management role?
- What would you bring to this team?
- What hard and soft skills matter most in this role?

### Client / relationship
- Tell me about a difficult client and how you handled it.
- Tell me about a time you had to manage expectations.
- How do you build trust with a technical and business stakeholder at the same institution?
- What do you do when the client is convinced your system caused the issue but the evidence is incomplete?
- How do you maintain proactive relationships rather than only reacting to tickets?

### Data / technical
- Walk me through a data-delivery workflow end to end.
- A research delivery is late. How do you investigate it?
- How would you use SQL to identify which providers are most frequently late?
- Where would Python help in this role?
- What would you automate, and what would you keep under human review?
- How would you distinguish a data-quality issue from a transport/system issue?

### Account / business
- Why would a research provider benefit from distributing through Bloomberg?
- How would you create a strategy for an account whose content has weak presence or inconsistent delivery?
- What metrics would you track for your portfolio?
- How would you use business intelligence to identify content gaps or product opportunities?

### Prioritization / ownership
- You have five open client issues and two implementation projects. What do you do first?
- Tell me about a time you took initiative without being told exactly what to do.
- Tell me about a project where something went wrong and you still followed through.
- How do you keep tickets and stakeholders updated when the final fix depends on another team?

### Collaboration
- Tell me about a time you translated between a technical person and a nontechnical client.
- How do you handle disagreement with Engineering or Product?
- What information does another team need from you for an efficient escalation?
